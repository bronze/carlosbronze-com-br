## The itch

The [federation's website](https://kravmaga.com.br/) was heavy, slow and hard to read. The menu blurred its sections together, and the cards had low-contrast text and were only clickable on a thin red strip.

<Shot name="fsakm-before" alt="The homepage of the federation's original site, top to bottom, in two columns: a large banner with the logo, a menu open on top of it, blocks of small text and dark cards with a red strip, a photo carousel, testimonials and the footer." caption="The federation's original site in March 2026, the whole homepage, in two columns:" source="https://web.archive.org/web/20260305134035/https://kravmaga.com.br/" />

## The insight

A real site with real problems was the chance I wanted to go deeper into Next.js, accessibility and componentization with shadcn/ui. Better than any tutorial: if it worked, the result would be visible to the people who use the site.

## What I did

I crawled the site and found around 500 pages organized into sections, almost like documentation. So instead of building everything from scratch, I looked for a documentation tool, which would already handle navigation, search and languages. I compared Docusaurus, Fumadocs and Nextra, and picked Nextra 4: it's built on Next.js, has native internationalization and search with Pagefind.

I kept the original site's sections. I only tidied up the URLs for instructors and gyms, which were scattered, into a clearer format: `/federacao/instrutores/name` and `/academias/country/state/gym`.

## What I learned

> Starting with content, not components, shows you which components you actually need.

One thing still bugs me: the mandatory `/pt` in Portuguese URLs. If I pick the project back up, that's the first fix.
