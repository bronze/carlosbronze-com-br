## The itch

I started my career as a planning trainee at NBS, the agency founded by Jurandir Craveiro. Years later, he [wrote on his blog](https://www.jura.com.br/premio-gp-formadores) that he had dug up Thompson Total Branding, JWT's planning methodology that was never rolled out and only existed on a CD-ROM. He spent over a year turning it into a website, and my name was on the list of the team he thanked.

It was rare material that a lot of planners relied on. And the site went offline.

## The insight

I wasn't involved in the original site, but I thought it would be worth getting involved. Organizing this content in a modern way would be a way to study the methodology and, at the same time, work out how to present it. Studying the content and deciding how to show it became the same job.

## What I did

The original site was gone, but a [backup survived on the Internet Archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20180716190000/http://jura.com.br/jwt/Fset001.htm). From there, I converted the pages to Markdown one by one. Since then, this manual has been my starting point whenever I want to try a new technology:

- 2019: [Gridsome](https://github.com/bronze/jamdocs-ttb), with the Jamdocs theme
- 2021: [VitePress](https://github.com/bronze/vitepress-starter)
- 2023: [Astro Starlight](https://github.com/bronze/ttb)
- 2025: [Fumadocs](https://github.com/bronze/ttb-fumadocs), built on Next.js, the version live today

It's over 50 pages, organized around the manual's Planning Cycle and its original questions: *Where are we now? Why are we there? Where could we be? How can we get there? Are we getting there?*

## The result

- The manual is back online: over 50 pages, easy to navigate from start to finish.
- Four tools tried over six years, always with the same content, which makes the comparison fair.
- I studied a classic planning methodology in depth and learned Fumadocs in a real context.

## What I learned

Some material doesn't age. A manual written decades ago still works as a bridge to learning what's new.
