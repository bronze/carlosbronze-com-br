## The itch

Rory Sutherland is one of the people who most changed how I think about marketing and behaviour, and I enjoy listening to him, even when he retells a story I already know. And he retells a lot: the same stories come back in different talks and interviews, a little different each time, scattered across YouTube in videos an hour long or more. I wanted an index of that. An encyclopedia of his stories, showing where he told each one and at what minute.

## The insight

You can't search video, but you can search text. With every talk transcribed into plain markdown, you can search for a word across 32 hours of content, and you can ask Claude to read all of it at once and find patterns: which stories repeat, which books come up, which ideas run through the talks.

## What I did

I built the site with Astro, using a YouTube playlist as the source. A Python script uses yt-dlp to download each video's metadata and subtitles and save them as a transcript. On top of that, Claude wrote the key takeaways for each talk and indexed the stories. The site offers four ways to browse:

Only some of the talks have had their stories cataloged so far. The most retold one, for now, is the Eurostar story: instead of spending billions to make the journey shorter, pay supermodels to serve vintage wine to every passenger, for a fraction of the money. It shows up in four talks.

## What I learned

> Design, code and data were the easy part. Understanding the content was the hard part.

Building the site, fetching the transcripts and organizing topics and books went fast. Cataloging the stories turned out to be much harder than I expected. To find an anecdote, Claude has to read the whole transcript, understand the context and recognize that it's the same story told differently in another talk. That costs a lot of tokens per talk, which is why the catalog is still half done.

Having the content as text was necessary, but not enough. The expensive part isn't storing it, it's interpreting it.
